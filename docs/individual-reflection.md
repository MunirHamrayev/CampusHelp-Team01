# Individual Reflection and Accountability – Munir Xammrayev

## What I was responsible for

I completed this project alone, so I was accountable for all three roles: Project Manager (T01, T04, T08, T10), Business Analyst & Designer (T02, T05, T07, T12) and Quality & Risk Coordinator (T03, T06, T09, T11). Every Issue, branch, commit and Pull Request in the repository is from my account. I reviewed each Pull Request against the quality checklist before merging.

## What went well

- The charter's explicit out-of-scope list made the change-request decision on Day 6 straightforward. I could show that email was excluded and defer it with evidence rather than opinion.
- Keeping a strict Definition of Done meant my progress figures were honest. On Day 2 the board showed 16.7%, not 25%, which told me early that I was behind.
- Writing the test cases straight from the requirements gave a clear pass/fail check for all six features.

## What I would do differently

- I underestimated design work and the cost of switching between roles. Next time I would level the workload during planning, not after the first slip.
- I would test each screen as soon as it was built, instead of leaving all testing to Day 7.
- The biggest weakness of a one-person project is the lack of independent review. Cross-role review against a written checklist helped, but a real second reviewer would catch things I cannot see in my own work.

## Accountability

The late M1 milestone (half a day) and the 4-hour effort overrun were caused by my own estimates. Both were recovered within the 10-day schedule and the budget reserve.
